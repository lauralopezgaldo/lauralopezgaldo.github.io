Selected Research

I study how neural population dynamics encode and transform internal and external information, using electrophysiology, signal processing, and machine learning to uncover the low-dimensional representations underlying behavior.

Motor cortex multiplexing

How can cortical columns encode multiple variables simultaneously?

Using laminar electrodes in motor cortex, we investigated how task variables are represented across cortical layers. We found that the neural subspaces encoding these variables were spatially distributed, with contributions from all layers. These subspaces remained distinct, preserving separate representations, but were not fully orthogonal, allowing them to interact.

Laminar oscillatory dynamics

How are beta oscillations organized across cortical layers?

Beta oscillations are predominant in the motor cortex. By analyzing laminar intracortical data, we found that such oscillatory events span across all layers, rather than being restricted to deep ones. Moreover, events emerging at similar frequencies co-occurred along the column, pointing towards column-wide generation of beta oscillations rather than multiple localized oscillators.
